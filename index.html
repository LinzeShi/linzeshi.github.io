About

I work on LLM inference and the infrastructure around it. Most of my time goes into vLLM, the open-source inference engine, and vLLM-Omni, its multimodal extension. The thread running through it is making serving work well for what comes after it: training loops, agents, and retrieval.

Before that I was at Meta, working on agent systems and how to run them efficiently.

Inference systems, and the research questions they raise, are what I spend my time on. My background is in computer science at Carnegie Mellon University, and I did my undergraduate studies at Xi'an Jiaotong University.

Oct 2026 – now

Teacher serving for on-policy distillation · vLLM core

In on-policy distillation a teacher scores every token the student writes, and scoring requests used to skip the prefix cache and recompute the shared context. I designed bounded prefix reuse across the KV cache manager and scheduler, including chunked prefill and preemption: 3.7–3.8× faster teacher scoring on long shared contexts with a 32B teacher at TP=4.

Late chunking · vLLM pooling

Brought late chunking into vLLM's pooling path: one pass over a long document, chunk vectors pooled on the GPU before the embedding head, so each chunk carries the context around it. Designed through an RFC, built for offline use and then extended to the online /v1/embeddings API.

On-policy distillation ICLR 2027 · ACL Rolling Review
Joint work, three papers
A line of work on how a student should learn from a teacher on its own samples. When a student matches a teacher's hidden states layer by layer, the last few layers end up dominating the gradient, and leaving them out of the loss works better than every reweighting we tested. A model's own expert persona can serve as its teacher, so distillation needs no reference solutions. And a teacher with a different tokenizer that exposes only its ranked top candidates, never its probabilities, still carries enough signal to teach. It's the same problem I work on from the serving side in vLLM, where scoring every student token is the cost the teacher-serving work brings down.
Reinforcement learning for native-language reasoning ACL Rolling Review
First author
Reasoning vision-language models think in English even for questions in other languages, and RL cannot reward a behavior it never samples. Supplying it during rollouts can make native reasoning the default.
